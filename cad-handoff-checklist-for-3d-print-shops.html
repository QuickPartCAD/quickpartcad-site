Minimum viable brief

Eight fields are enough for most small jobs to be scoped

The goal is not to turn the print shop into a CAD department. QuickPartCAD only needs the information that controls geometry, fit, output, and commercial handoff. Everything else can stay in the shop's normal customer workflow.

Handoff checklist

What to include when forwarding a customer CAD job

FieldWhat to sendWhy it matters
1. One-sentence job goal“Make a bracket that mounts sensor X to rail Y.”Defines the functional problem before geometry details distract from it.
2. Customer referencesPhotos, sketch, broken part photos, STL/STEP, PDF, or existing CADShows the starting geometry and the customer's intent.
3. Fit-critical dimensionsHole diameters, spacing, mating sizes, offsets, clearancesLets the model be driven by measurable interfaces.
4. Print contextFDM process, material, nozzle/layer assumptions if already fixedHelps avoid geometry that conflicts with the shop's actual process.
5. Load/use noteLight positioning, general functional use, moderate load, heat/outdoor exposureSets the practical design context without pretending to be formal FEA.
6. Required deliverablesSTL, 3MF, STEP, editable CAD, or a combinationPrevents a correct model from arriving in the wrong handoff format.
7. DeadlineNeeded for quote, prototype, or customer deliveryAllows the scope and turnaround to be agreed before CAD starts.
8. Communication boundary“Work through our shop” or “direct clarification is OK”Keeps the print shop in control of its customer relationship.

When to ask the customer one more question

Four gaps that are worth resolving before CAD starts

Gap 1

No dimension controls scale

A photo-only request needs at least one reliable measurement, and usually more at the mating interface.

Gap 2

The model must fit something not shown

Ask for the mating object's dimensions or a photo that clearly shows the interface.

Gap 3

The customer says “same, but bigger”

Clarify which dimensions scale and which must stay fixed, especially holes, wall thickness, and hardware.

Gap 4

The required output is unclear

Ask whether the shop needs only a print file or also STEP/editable CAD for later revisions.

FAQ

Short answers

Does a print shop need a formal drawing before sending a CAD job?

No. For the work QuickPartCAD targets, a forwarded customer email, marked photos or sketch, key dimensions, and the required output format can be enough to start scoping.

Who communicates with the end customer?

By default, the print shop can remain the customer-facing party. QuickPartCAD can work from the brief the shop provides unless direct clarification is explicitly requested.

What should the shop decide before sending the job?

At minimum: what the part must fit or do, the dimensions that control fit, the intended printing process/material if known, and which files are required back.

What does QuickPartCAD return?

The agreed CAD/print files, typically STEP, STL, 3MF, and/or editable CAD depending on the job, plus a clear handoff suitable for the shop to print or deliver to its customer.

Want to test the workflow with one real customer job?

Forward one customer request using the checklist above. We will scope that job first; no retainer or ongoing arrangement is required.

QuickPartCAD measurement guide

How to photograph and measure a broken part for replacement CAD.

Use photos to remove shape ambiguity and measure the interfaces that control fit. This is the evidence checklist we use when scoping simple replacement parts from photos or broken originals.

Minimum useful evidence

A photo explains shape; measurements explain fit

For the replacement parts QuickPartCAD handles, we prioritize interface dimensions over a complete drawing. If the part must fit a shaft, hole pattern, slot, housing, rail, screw, or mating face, those dimensions matter more than documenting every cosmetic edge.

Photo checklist

The seven views we ask for when they are available

You do not always need all seven. The goal is to remove ambiguity, not to create a photography project.

  1. Overall front view. Shows the basic outline and major features.
  2. Overall back view. Reveals hidden bosses, ribs, mounting faces, or recesses.
  3. Top or plan view. Helps show hole patterns, offsets, and symmetry.
  4. Side view. Establishes thickness, steps, angles, and depth.
  5. Mating-area close-up. Show exactly where the replacement part touches or attaches to another object.
  6. Broken or missing feature close-up. Include the fracture and the neighboring intact geometry.
  7. In-context photo. Show the part where it is used, especially if orientation or load direction matters.

If you only have time for three measurements

Measure the dimensions that can make the part fail to fit

Priority A

Mating size

The diameter, width, slot, rail, shaft, board, or surface the part must fit around or into.

Priority B

Mounting location

Hole center-to-center spacing, edge offsets, or the relative position between interfaces.

Priority C

Available envelope

The maximum width, height, depth, or clearance the new part can occupy without colliding with something else.

Useful extra

Scale reference in a photo

A ruler or caliper visible in the same plane as the feature can help interpret the image, but it does not replace direct measurements for tight fits.

FAQ

Short answers

Can you make an exact replacement part from one photo?

Usually not from one photo alone. A photo shows shape and context, but exact fit normally needs at least a few measurements or a known scale reference. For simple parts, a small set of fit-critical dimensions can be enough.

What should I measure first?

Measure the interfaces first: holes, shafts, slots, mating faces, center-to-center spacing, and the overall envelope. Cosmetic dimensions can usually wait until the fit is understood.

Do I need digital calipers?

They are helpful for small features and tight fits, but not mandatory for every part. A ruler can be sufficient for larger non-critical geometry. Better measurement tools matter most at the interfaces.

What if part of the original is broken off?

Photograph the broken part and the object it mates with. Dimensions from the mating object can often reconstruct the missing interface more reliably than guessing the broken geometry.

Have a broken or missing part?

Send the photos you already have plus the key measurements. We will tell you what is sufficient and what single measurement would remove the biggest uncertainty.

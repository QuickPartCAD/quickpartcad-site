Start with the useful information

What to send, and what you get back

You do not need a formal engineering drawing. A clear sketch, photos, and the dimensions that control fit are often enough.

What to send

  • Your starting point: sketch, photo, reference image, rough drawing, or a few notes.
  • The critical dimensions: what must fit, align, clear, or mount to something else.
  • The intended use: what the part holds, connects, protects, spaces, or moves.
  • Print constraints if known: material, printer/process, preferred orientation, fasteners, or assembly method.

What you get

  • Functional CAD geometry designed around the interfaces and dimensions that matter.
  • STEP, STL, 3MF, and/or editable CAD depending on what you need for printing or later changes.
  • FDM-aware details such as practical wall thickness, clearance, hole sizing, and simple support-conscious geometry.
  • A compact design process aimed at getting a usable part rather than producing unnecessary documentation.

FAQ

Questions people ask before sending a CAD job

Can I order CAD for a single one-off 3D printed part?

Yes. Small one-off functional parts are a good fit when you can describe the geometry, dimensions and intended use.

Can you design a 3D-printable part from a hand sketch?

Yes. A hand sketch is a good starting point if it shows the overall intent. Add dimensions for the features that must fit or align with real hardware.

What file formats can you deliver?

Typical deliverables are STEP, STL, 3MF, and editable CAD when agreed. The exact set depends on whether you need only a print file or also future editability.

Can you make the CAD editable?

Yes, for projects where editable CAD is part of the scope. Tell us which CAD workflow you plan to use so we can choose a practical handoff.

Do you design specifically for FDM printing?

Yes. We consider practical FDM issues such as wall thickness, assembly clearance, hole behavior, orientation, and simple overhang/support implications.

Do you provide FEA or certified engineering calculations?

No. QuickPartCAD is a focused CAD service for functional parts and 3D printing. Load-critical or regulated designs that require formal analysis or certification should be reviewed by the appropriate engineer.

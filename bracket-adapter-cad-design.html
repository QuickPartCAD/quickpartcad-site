Start with the useful information

What to send, and what you get back

You do not need a formal engineering drawing. A clear sketch, photos, and the dimensions that control fit are often enough.

What to send

  • Both mating interfaces: photos or sketches showing what the bracket connects.
  • Mounting dimensions: hole diameters, center-to-center spacing, shaft sizes, slot widths, or clamp diameters.
  • Relative position: offset, angle, height, orientation, and any no-go/clearance zones.
  • Hardware details: screw size, nut/insert type, countersink/counterbore preference, or existing fasteners.

What you get

  • A bracket or adapter model built around the real mounting geometry you provide.
  • FDM-ready details such as practical wall/rib geometry, fastener access, and print-conscious clearances.
  • STEP, STL, 3MF, and/or editable CAD depending on your workflow.
  • A clear interface-driven design that can be updated later if one side of the mounting pattern changes.

FAQ

Questions people ask before sending a CAD job

Can you design a bracket from just the hole spacing?

Sometimes, but the best result includes the full interface: hole size, spacing, part envelope, offset/angle, available clearance, and the fasteners you plan to use.

Can you add slots, countersinks, or counterbores?

Yes. Provide the screw or hardware size and the adjustment range or head dimensions you need.

Can you make an adapter between two existing parts?

Yes. This is a typical job. Send the dimensions of both mating sides and how you want them positioned relative to each other.

Can you design a load-bearing bracket?

We can model functional bracket geometry based on the load and constraints you provide, but QuickPartCAD does not provide FEA, certification, or a guarantee for safety-critical structural use.

What is the minimum information you need?

A simple sketch plus the mounting dimensions on both sides is often enough to start. Photos are useful for checking surrounding clearance and assembly access.

Start with the useful information

What to send, and what you get back

You do not need a formal engineering drawing. A clear sketch, photos, and the dimensions that control fit are often enough.

What to send

  • Photos from several angles: front, side, top, and the mating area if possible.
  • Critical dimensions: overall size plus the holes, tabs, shafts, slots, or surfaces that must fit another part.
  • A scale reference: ruler, caliper reading, or one known dimension visible in the photo.
  • What the part does: tell us what it mounts, supports, covers, spaces, or connects.

What you get

  • A functional 3D model built around the dimensions that matter for fit and use.
  • Print-ready files such as STL or 3MF, plus STEP or editable CAD when useful or requested.
  • Reasonable FDM details such as wall thickness, clearances, holes, and simple assembly features.
  • A model you can revise later when editable CAD is part of the agreed deliverable.

FAQ

Questions people ask before sending a CAD job

Can you recreate a replacement part from one photo?

A photo can be a starting point, but accurate CAD usually requires scale and fit-critical measurements, ideally from multiple views or the mating part.

Can you make an STL from a photo?

Yes, for simple functional parts when the shape can be understood and the important dimensions are known. A photo gives visual reference; measurements establish the actual size and fit.

What dimensions do I need?

Start with the overall width, height, and depth, then measure every interface that must fit something else: hole diameters and spacing, slot sizes, shaft diameters, mating gaps, and mounting surfaces.

Can you work from a broken part?

Yes. Photos of the broken part and the place where it fits are useful. If a feature is missing, include measurements from the mating object so we can reconstruct the intended interface.

Do I need calipers?

Calipers are helpful for small mechanical features, but they are not mandatory for every job. A ruler can be enough for larger, less critical geometry. Fit-critical features benefit from better measurements.

Will the first print always fit perfectly?

We model to the dimensions and fit requirements you provide, but printers, materials, and real-world measurements vary. For tight interfaces, a small test print or one revision may be the sensible final step.
